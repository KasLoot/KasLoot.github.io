PrefMem

A preference-aware, evidence-gated agentic layer that turns ambiguous instructions into confirmed, monitored robot tasks, without training a new policy.

The problem

Vision-language-action models map language and camera images to robot actions, but what a person wants often lies beyond a single instruction. Current VLAs also tend to replay training trajectories rather than follow the semantics of a new instruction, especially on long-horizon tasks in new environments. Recent fixes, such as SemanticVLA and MeM, change the model architecture and need retraining, and neither addresses understanding the user's intent.

PrefMem takes a different path. Instead of training a new policy, it adds an orchestration layer above an interchangeable action executor. Five model-facing agents handle conversation, preference memory, planning, step monitoring and final validation. A deterministic runtime and receding-horizon controller own everything that must not drift: goal identity, confirmation, task publication, state transitions, evidence rules and bounded recovery.

Limitation I'm taking forward. Across 30 interactions with related edits, a focal preference was still recalled at the end in only 2 of 8 memory stores: free-text identity let the system rewrite the wrong record. It points to immutable, structured keys for user, task, preference dimension and provenance.
